# Collecting a small claims judgment — research memo

Date checked: **2026-11-05**. Legal information, not legal advice. Each claim has its source URL right after it. Anything confirmed only by a secondary source or not confirmed at all is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

Abbreviations: CCP = California Code of Civil Procedure; Gov. Code = California Government Code; leginfo = leginfo.legislature.ca.gov.

## Part 1. California

### 1.1 When you can start collecting

- Enforcement of a small claims judgment, including recording an abstract of judgment, is **automatically suspended until the time to appeal runs out**, with no bond required. If an appeal is filed, enforcement stays suspended until the appeal is dismissed or decided. — CCP 116.810, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.810
- The appeal deadline is **30 days** after the clerk delivers or mails the Notice of Entry of Judgment. — CCP 116.750(b), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.750
- The Notice of Entry of Judgment (form SC-130, Rev. Jan. 1, 2025) states: "Enforcement of the judgment is automatically postponed for 30 days or, if an appeal is filed, until the appeal is decided." — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc130.pdf
- Self-help: wait at least 30 days after the Notice of Entry of Judgment was handed or mailed to you. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-your-money-if-you-won-your-small-claims-case
- A defendant who was never properly served has up to **180 days** after learning of the judgment to move to vacate, and the court may suspend enforcement while that motion is pending. — CCP 116.740, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.740
- **The court does not collect for you.** SC-130 says, in capitals, that the court will not collect the money or enforce the judgment for you (https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc130.pdf). The self-help page warns that collecting "can take a lot of time and money and you may not even collect any of it" (https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-your-money-if-you-won-your-small-claims-case).
- Lawyers may represent either side in enforcement, even though they can't appear at the small claims hearing. — CCP 116.530(c)(4), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.530

**How the debtor can pay:**
- Directly to the creditor, or to the court using SC-145. Paying through the court costs the debtor a $20 fee. If the debtor pays by personal check, the court waits 30 days before entering satisfaction. — CCP 116.840, 116.860, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.860
- The court can order installment payments at judgment or later on motion (SC-220). The creditor responds on SC-221; self-help says within 10 days. — CCP 116.620, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.620; https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-your-money-if-you-won-your-small-claims-case

### 1.2 Interest

- The standard rate is **10% per year** on the unpaid principal. — CCP 685.010(a)(1), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=685.010
- The rate drops to **5% per year** for judgments entered (or renewed) on or after Jan. 1, 2023 when the debtor is a natural person and either:
  - the claim is for medical expenses and the principal is under $200,000, or
  - the claim is for personal debt (personal, family, or household purposes) and the principal is under $50,000.

  Tort, fraud, and wage claims don't count as "due or owing" for this rule. — CCP 685.010(a)(2), same URL
- Self-help also lists **7%** when the debtor is a government agency. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/judgment-renewals-and-interest-rates
- SC-130 item 10 is where the court marks which portion of the judgment is medical or personal debt. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc130.pdf
- Post-judgment costs and accrued interest are added using form MC-012 (Rev. Jan. 1, 2026). — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/mc012.pdf; https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-writ-execution
- The prevailing party is entitled to the costs of enforcing the judgment and to accrued interest. — CCP 116.820(c), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.820

### 1.3 How long a judgment lasts, and renewal

- A money judgment is enforceable for **10 years** from entry. After that, enforcement stops and liens are extinguished. — CCP 683.020, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=683.020
- Renewal must be filed **before the 10-year period expires**. — CCP 683.130(a), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=683.130
- A judgment can't be renewed again within 5 years of a prior renewal. — CCP 683.110(b), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=683.110
- Medical-expense judgments under $200,000 and personal-debt judgments under $50,000 against a natural person **can be renewed only once**. — CCP 683.110(c), same URL
- Self-help says that one renewal lasts **5 years**, and that renewals for other judgments last 10 years with no limit on the number. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/judgment-renewals-and-interest-rates
- The renewal fee is **$45**. — Gov. Code 70626(b)(4); Statewide Civil Fee Schedule eff. Jan. 1, 2026, item 70, https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/statewide-civil-fee-schedule-eff-01012026.pdf

### 1.4 Finding assets: Statement of Assets (SC-133) and the debtor's exam (SC-134)

**SC-133, Judgment Debtor's Statement of Assets (Rev. Jan. 1, 2011)**
- Unless the judgment has been paid, the debtor must fill out SC-133 and deliver it to the creditor within **30 days** after the clerk mails the notice of entry of judgment.
- If the debtor moved to vacate or appealed, the 30 days run from the denial or the appeal result.
- If the debtor willfully fails to do this, the creditor can ask the court for sanctions under CCP 708.170, including arrest and attorney's fees.
- Source: CCP 116.830, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.830. The form itself says "MAIL TO THE JUDGMENT CREDITOR. DO NOT FILE WITH THE COURT." — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc133.pdf

**SC-134, Application and Order to Produce Statement of Assets and to Appear for Examination (Rev. Jan. 1, 2025)**
- This orders the debtor to bring a completed SC-133 and answer questions under oath.
- The order must be served **at least 30 days before the exam**. Service is personal, by a sheriff, marshal, or registered process server.
- The debtor must live or do business within **150 miles** of the place of examination.
- Sources: https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc134.pdf; https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-other-side-answer-questions-about-their-income-and-property

**Related forms and costs**
- If the debtor already gave you SC-133, use EJ-125 instead (Rev. Oct. 1, 2024). — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej125.pdf
- If SC-130 marks the judgment as consumer debt, use SC-136 (Rev. Jan. 1, 2026). — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc136.pdf
- Filing fee: **$60**. — Fee Schedule item 94, https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/statewide-civil-fee-schedule-eff-01012026.pdf
- Sheriff service: self-help says "usually around $40". — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-other-side-answer-questions-about-their-income-and-property
- You can subpoena records such as bank statements and pay stubs with SC-107. — same page; https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc107.pdf
- If the debtor is more than 150 miles away: get an Abstract of Judgment (EJ-001) and file EJ-125 in the debtor's county. — same self-help page

### 1.5 Writ of execution (EJ-130)

- A writ of execution is required before the sheriff or levying officer can garnish wages or levy a bank account. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-writ-execution
- The clerk's fee is **$40**, and it can be added to the judgment. — Gov. Code 70626(a)(1); Fee Schedule item 95, https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/statewide-civil-fee-schedule-eff-01012026.pdf
- **The writ expires in 180 days.** — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-writ-execution
- EJ-130 was revised July 1, 2026. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej130.pdf
- SC-130 lists examples of property the officer may take: wages, bank account, automobile, business property, rental income. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc130.pdf

### 1.6 Wage garnishment (earnings withholding order)

**Current rule (operative since Sept. 1, 2023)**

The most that can be withheld from an individual's **disposable earnings** for a workweek is the **lesser of**:
1. **20%** of disposable earnings, or
2. **40% of the amount by which weekly disposable earnings exceed 48 × the state minimum hourly wage**.

If the debtor works where the local minimum wage is higher than the state minimum, the local rate is used. For other pay periods, the multipliers are 96 hours (biweekly), 104 hours (semimonthly), and 208 hours (monthly).

Source: CCP 706.050, enacted by SB 1477, Stats. 2022, ch. 849, effective Jan. 1, 2023, operative Sept. 1, 2023. — https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=706.050

So the rule in the brief ("lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or 40% of the amount over 48× state minimum wage") is **correct**.

**State minimum wage for 2026: $16.90/hour for all employers.** — California DIR, https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_minimumwage.htm

**Worked figures** (my arithmetic from the statute and the $16.90 wage; local minimum wages will change these):

| Pay period | Protected floor (hours × $16.90) | Nothing can be taken below | 20% cap takes over above |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | 48 × 16.90 = **$811.20** | $811.20 | $1,622.40 |
| Biweekly | 96 × 16.90 = $1,622.40 | $1,622.40 | $3,244.80 |
| Semimonthly | 104 × 16.90 = $1,757.60 | $1,757.60 | $3,515.20 |
| Monthly | 208 × 16.90 = $3,515.20 | $3,515.20 | $7,030.40 |

- Example: weekly disposable earnings of $1,000 → 40% × ($1,000 − $811.20) = **$75.52/week** (20% would be $200, so the lower figure applies).
- The CA self-help page simplifies this to "up to 20% of the debtor's gross income." That is not how the statute is worded: it uses disposable earnings and a minimum-wage formula. Use the statute. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-money-someones-paycheck

**Process**
1. Get the writ (EJ-130).
2. Give the levying officer the original writ and the WG-001 Application for Earnings Withholding Order (Rev. Jan. 1, 2012). The officer serves WG-002 on the employer.
3. The employer returns WG-005 within 15 days.
4. For personal-debt judgments, also give the officer WG-015/EJ-135 (address verification).
5. The debtor may file a claim of exemption, for example if the money is needed for support. The creditor can oppose (WG-009/WG-010).
6. If the debtor changes jobs, you start over with the new employer.

Sources: https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-money-someones-paycheck; https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/wg001.pdf

**Fees and priority**
- The sheriff's fee for an earnings withholding order is **$45**, covering all of the levying officer's duties for that levy. — Gov. Code 26750, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=26750
- Tax and child-support withholding take priority over a small claims judgment. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-money-someones-paycheck

### 1.7 Bank levy and the automatic bank-account exemption

**The automatic exemption**
- Money in the debtor's deposit account is **exempt without making a claim** up to the "minimum basic standard of adequate care for a family of four for Region 1" (Welf. & Inst. Code 11452). CDSS adjusts this every year.
- It applies **per debtor, not per account**.
- It does not apply to levies for wages owed, child or spousal support, or state tax agencies.
- Source: CCP 704.220 (operative Sept. 1, 2020; last amended by AB 2837, eff. Jan. 1, 2025), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=704.220

**Current amount: $2,325 per judgment debtor, effective July 1, 2026.** It is adjusted every July 1. — Judicial Council form EJ-156 (Rev. July 20, 2026), https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej156.pdf. The previous EJ-156 showed $2,170 (seen in search results only; not opened).

**Direct-deposited benefits protected without a claim** (CCP 704.080, amounts from EJ-156):

| Benefit type | One designated payee | Two or more designated payees |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security | $4,400 | $6,575 |
| Other public benefits | $2,175 | $3,250 |

Amounts above these are still exempt to the extent they are traceable to the benefits. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej156.pdf

**Process**
1. Get the writ.
2. Find the bank's designated levy branch. Banks with 10 or more branches use a central location.
3. Give the sheriff the writ, written instructions (SER-001/SER-001A), and the fee. Self-help says the fee is "usually around $50.00"; the statutory service fee is $50 (Gov. Code 26721, 26720.9: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=26720.9).
4. A levy catches only the balance on the day the bank is served, so timing matters.
5. The debtor has 15–20 days to claim exemptions. The creditor can oppose with EJ-170/EJ-175 within 15 days.

Source: https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/collect-money-bank-account

### 1.8 Exemptions (what can't be taken)

Current amounts from EJ-156 (Rev. July 20, 2026); most are effective April 1, 2025 and adjust every 3 years (CCP 703.150(e)). — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej156.pdf

| Property | Exemption | Source section |
|---|---|---|
| Motor vehicle (equity) | **$8,625** | CCP 704.010 |
| Tools of trade, business or professional property (one spouse) | **$10,950** (commercial vehicle up to $4,850) | CCP 704.060 |
| Tools of trade (both spouses in same trade) | $21,900 (commercial vehicle up to $9,700) | CCP 704.060 |
| Jewelry, heirlooms, art | $10,950 | CCP 704.040 |
| Bank account (automatic) | $2,325 | CCP 704.220 |
| Life insurance loan value | $17,525 | CCP 704.100 |
| Accrued vacation, sick, or family leave | $8,625 | CCP 704.113 |

- **Homestead (the debtor's home):** the greater of **$300,000** or the countywide median sale price for a single-family home in the prior year, **capped at $600,000**. Both figures adjust every year for inflation (California CPI) beginning Jan. 1, 2022. — CCP 704.730, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=704.730. The current inflation-adjusted dollar figures for 2026 were **not found** in a primary source (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **Public benefits:** EJ-155 (Rev. Sept. 1, 2021) lists the full set of exempt property, including Social Security, SSI, unemployment, and workers' comp. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej155.pdf. Federal benefits are also protected at the bank under 31 C.F.R. Part 212 (see Part 2).
- Self-help notes the homestead exemption generally blocks foreclosing on the debtor's own home, and foreclosure of a judgment lien isn't available for consumer debt. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/put-lien-property

### 1.9 Abstract of judgment and liens

**Real property**
- Recording an **Abstract of Judgment (EJ-001, Rev. July 1, 2014)** with the county recorder creates a judgment lien on the debtor's real property in that county. — CCP 697.310(a), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=697.310
- The lien lasts **10 years from entry of judgment**, extended by renewal. — CCP 697.310(b), same URL
- The court fee for the abstract is **$40**, plus the county recorder's fee. — Fee Schedule item 96, https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/statewide-civil-fee-schedule-eff-01012026.pdf; https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/put-lien-property
- Record one abstract in each county where the debtor owns property. You get paid when the property is sold or refinanced. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/put-lien-property
- The abstract can't be issued or recorded during the 30-day stay. — CCP 116.810(a)

**Personal property**
- A judgment lien on certain business personal property can be created by filing a notice with the **California Secretary of State**. It lasts **5 years**, and a continuation can be filed in the 6 months before it lapses. — CCP 697.510, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=697.510

### 1.10 Till taps, keeper levies, and other business levies

- At the creditor's direction, the levying officer can place a **keeper** in charge of a business.
  - The business keeps operating, but sales must be final and for cash or the equivalent, and the keeper takes the proceeds.
  - Exclusive custody of tangible property follows if the debtor objects, if the creditor asks, or after 10 days.
  - If only cash or sales proceeds are being taken, custody is at the end of each daily keeper period. This is the "till tap" style levy.
  - Source: CCP 700.070, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=700.070
- Keeper fees: **$175 per 8-hour period** (no more than $350 per 24 hours per keeper), plus $50/day of custody after the first day. — Gov. Code 26726, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=26726
- Self-help lists till tap, keeper, and third-party levies (for example, a tenant's rent) as "less common" methods that "can be expensive." It also mentions court-ordered turnover of valuable personal property. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-collect-small-claims-case

### 1.11 Driver's license suspension for vehicle-accident judgments

- Every small claims judgment must state whether it resulted from a motor vehicle accident on a California highway caused by the defendant's driving, or by a vehicle registered to the defendant. — CCP 116.610(c), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.610
- **Over $1,000 property damage, or any bodily injury or death:** the Vehicle Code financial-responsibility suspension provisions (Veh. Code 16250–16381) apply. — CCP 116.870, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.870
- **$1,000 or less, unpaid more than 90 days after the judgment became final:** the creditor can file a notice with the DMV, with a DMV fee, asking for suspension. DMV notifies the debtor, and the suspension lasts **90 days**, starting 20 days after DMV receives the notice. It ends early on proof of payment, a court payment schedule, insurance coverage, or a deposit. — CCP 116.880, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.880
- Self-help also mentions holds on **professional licenses** for cases involving professional work. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-collect-small-claims-case. The specific statutes (for example, contractor licenses) were not checked (**UNVERIFIED**).

### 1.12 Recovering costs

- The prevailing party gets the costs of the action, including the cost of serving the defendant. — CCP 116.610(g), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.610
- The prevailing party also gets the costs of enforcing the judgment and accrued interest. — CCP 116.820(c), https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.820
- Claim enforcement costs on MC-012. Keep receipts; for example, the $60 debtor-exam fee can be recovered from the debtor. — https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-get-other-side-answer-questions-about-their-income-and-property; https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/how-collect-small-claims-case
- Exception: a frequent filer's extra fee (the $100 fee minus the normal tier) is not recoverable. — CCP 116.610(g)(2)

### 1.13 When you're paid: Acknowledgment of Satisfaction (SC-290)

- On full payment, the creditor must **immediately** file an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment.
- If the creditor fails to file within **14 days** after the debtor's written demand, without good cause, the creditor is liable for the debtor's damages **plus $50**.
- If the creditor won't sign, the debtor can get satisfaction entered by filing proof of payment with a declaration.
- Source: CCP 116.850, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=116.850
- Form SC-290 (Rev. July 1, 2025): https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc290.pdf
- If an abstract was recorded, use **EJ-100** (Rev. July 1, 2014), which can be recorded, instead. — https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/sc130.pdf; https://www.courts.ca.gov/documents/ej100.pdf

### 1.14 California collection cost cheat-sheet

All fees are from the Statewide Civil Fee Schedule eff. Jan. 1, 2026 (https://courts.ca.gov/system/files/file/statewide-civil-fee-schedule-eff-01012026.pdf) or the Gov. Code sections cited above.

| Step | Fee |
|---|---|
| Debtor's exam application | $60 + service (~$40–$50) |
| Writ of execution | $40 |
| Wage garnishment (levying officer) | $45 |
| Bank levy (sheriff) | $50 |
| Abstract of judgment | $40 + recorder fee |
| Renewal of judgment | $45 |
| Keeper | $175 per 8 hours |

Most of these can be added to the judgment as enforcement costs.

## Part 2. General / all states

### 2.1 Federal wage garnishment cap (Consumer Credit Protection Act)

- For ordinary debts, the most that can be garnished in a workweek is the **lesser of**:
  - **25% of disposable earnings**, or
  - the amount by which disposable earnings exceed **30 × the federal minimum wage**.
- Source: 15 U.S.C. § 1673(a). Statute text read from the Cornell LII mirror: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1673. The official uscode.house.gov page could not be retrieved on 2026-11-05.
- U.S. Department of Labor Fact Sheet #30 confirms the rule and the **$217.50/week** floor (30 × $7.25). Between $217.50 and $290, only the excess can be taken; at $290 or more, the 25% cap applies. — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa
- **When state law is stricter, the stricter (lower) amount controls.** That is why California's 20% / 48× rule governs in CA. — same DOL page
- Child support, alimony, bankruptcy orders, and tax debts follow different limits. — same DOL page

### 2.2 Federal benefits in bank accounts (31 C.F.R. Part 212)

- When a bank is served with a garnishment order, it must perform an **account review within two business days**. — 31 C.F.R. § 212.5(a), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-212/section-212.5
- The review looks for direct-deposited benefits from **SSA, VA, OPM, or the Railroad Retirement Board**. — 31 C.F.R. § 212.3, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-212/section-212.3
- **Lookback period:** "the two month period that begins on the date preceding the date of account review and ends on the corresponding date of the month two months earlier." — § 212.3
- **Protected amount:** the lesser of the sum of benefit payments posted during the lookback period, or the account balance at the time of review. The bank must leave this amount available to the debtor. — §§ 212.3, 212.6
- The review applies regardless of commingled funds, co-owners, or instructions in the order. — § 212.5(d)
- This protection applies in every state, on top of state exemptions such as California's $2,325 automatic exemption.

### 2.3 States that bar or sharply limit wage garnishment for ordinary debts

| State | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Texas** | "Except as otherwise provided by state or federal law, current wages for personal service are not subject to garnishment." (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 63.004) | https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/CP/htm/CP.63.htm |
| **Pennsylvania** | Wages, salaries, and commissions in the employer's hands are exempt from attachment or execution except in actions for support, divorce-related matters, board for 4 weeks or less, **residential lease judgments for landlords** (capped), PHEAA student loans, and criminal restitution/costs/fines (42 Pa.C.S. § 8127(a)). | https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/42/00.081.027.000..HTM |
| **North Carolina** | Earnings from the debtor's personal services within the 60 days before the order cannot be applied to the judgment if they are necessary for the use of a family supported wholly or partly by the debtor's labor (N.C.G.S. § 1-362). This is a family-support exemption, not an absolute ban. | https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_1/GS_1-362.html |
| **South Carolina** | For debts from a consumer credit sale, consumer lease, consumer loan, or consumer rental-purchase agreement, the creditor "may not attach unpaid earnings of the debtor by garnishment or like proceedings" (S.C. Code § 37-5-104). | https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c005.php |

Notes on the table:
- **Pennsylvania:** a landlord's small claims judgment for residential rent is one of the exceptions.
- **North Carolina:** the general wisdom that NC "doesn't allow wage garnishment for consumer debts" is **UNVERIFIED** as a blanket rule beyond this statute.
- **South Carolina:** the statute covers consumer-credit debts only. Whether SC courts allow wage garnishment for other small claims (magistrate court) judgments, such as property damage, was **not verified (UNVERIFIED)**.
- Other states with low caps or head-of-household protections (for example, Florida's head-of-family exemption) were **not checked** this round.

### 2.4 Typical collection tools in most states

These are the general patterns; procedures, names, and fees vary by state. The California versions are above and serve as the model.

- **Post-judgment discovery / debtor's examination.** Court-ordered questioning about income and assets. Failing to appear can lead to contempt or an arrest warrant (in CA, CCP 708.170 per CCP 116.830(d)).
- **Writ of execution / levy** on bank accounts and non-exempt personal property, carried out by a sheriff, marshal, or constable.
- **Wage garnishment**, capped by federal law and often lower under state law (see 2.1 and 2.3).
- **Judgment lien on real estate** by recording or docketing the judgment in the county.
- **Business levies** (till tap, keeper) and third-party levies on money owed to the debtor (rent, receivables).
- **Judgment renewal** before expiration. Judgment life varies by state (California: 10 years, renewable).
- **License sanctions** for certain judgments (California: driver's license for vehicle-accident judgments).

## Part 3. Odds of getting paid — what the evidence says

**Bottom line:** there is **no national or California dataset** on how often small claims winners actually collect. The best evidence is a few local studies, mostly old, plus one newspaper survey. Courts generally don't track collection. A Maine court statistician said the law didn't even allow the state to ask (see the Maine survey below).

### 3.1 Solid data (published studies with a stated method)

### 3.2 The Maine "two-thirds never collected" figure (anecdotal / journalistic)

- **Source:** Sun Journal (Lewiston, Maine), "Small claims smaller gains," Aug. 22, 2004. — https://www.sunjournal.com/2004/08/22/small-claims-smaller-gains/
- **What it says:** "An informal survey conducted by the Sun Journal found that of the hundreds of people each year who receive small claims judgments in their favor in Androscoggin, Franklin and Oxford counties, roughly two-thirds of them did not collect their money."
- District court clerks in the area estimated that winners got their money "only one-quarter to one-third of the time." The clerks also said they don't track payment.
- The state courts' statistician said the small claims law did not allow the state to ask for that information.
- **Classification:** this is a **newspaper's informal survey plus clerk estimates**, covering three counties. It is **not** a controlled study. The methodology beyond "informal survey" is behind a paywall and was not read. Treat it as anecdotal, but it is consistent with the Denver data.

### 3.3 Debt-collection small claims (context: who uses small claims and how cases end)

These studies are about creditors suing consumers. They describe **default rates and win rates**, not whether winners collect. They are relevant for (a) understanding the docket and (b) default-judgment risk.

**Pew Charitable Trusts, "How Philadelphia Municipal Court's Civil Division Works" (Feb. 10, 2021; updated Apr. 6, 2022)** — https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/reports/2021/02/how-philadelphia-municipal-courts-civil-division-works
- Method: 2013–2018 court data plus a May 2020 survey of 308 defendants.
- Plaintiffs won **97.6%** of small claims cases that ended in a judgment for one side.
- Nearly **46%** of judgments were defaults, and more than 99% of defaults went to the plaintiff.
- **91%** of plaintiffs had lawyers vs. about **12%** of defendants.
- **32%** of civil cases were dismissed for no service.
- 59% of surveyed defendants' cases involved credit card debt.

**Pew, "How Debt Collection Works in Philadelphia's Municipal Court" (Oct. 26, 2022)** — https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/reports/2022/10/how-debt-collection-works-in-philadelphias-municipal-court
- **Confirmed:** "Philadelphia Municipal Court data from 2013 to 2018 showed that the majority of the more than 100,000 small claims cases in Philadelphia courts were debt collection cases filed against individuals by creditors or debt collectors."
- The outcome table covers **117,117** cases:
  - 35% default judgment for plaintiff
  - 24% dismissed for no service
  - 1% judgment for plaintiff after trial
  - 1% judgment for defendant
- Of nearly 45,000 cases that reached a judgment, **95%** were defaults.
- Default rates were 69% in Detroit and 53% in Chicago.

**Pew, "How Debt Collectors Are Transforming the Business of State Courts" (May 6, 2020)** — https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/reports/2020/05/how-debt-collectors-are-transforming-the-business-of-state-courts
- Debt collection suits rose from under 1.7 million (1993) to about 4 million (2013), from about 1 in 9 civil cases to about 1 in 4.
- Courts resolved **more than 70%** of debt suits by default judgment in the jurisdictions with data.
- 75% of civil judgments in 2013 were under $5,200. Pew cites the National Center for State Courts' *Landscape of Civil Litigation in State Courts* (2015) for this. The NCSC report itself could not be retrieved (403), so the NCSC figures are cited through Pew.
- Debtors had lawyers in less than 10% of cases.

**Alexa Rosenbloom (Director, Harvard Law School Consumer Protection Clinic), "The Pervasive and Troubling Use of Coverage Attorneys in Assembly-Line Litigation," Georgetown Journal on Poverty Law & Policy, Vol. 33, Issue II (Winter 2026)** — https://www.law.georgetown.edu/poverty-journal/in-print/volume-33-issue-ii-winter-2026/the-pervasive-and-troubling-use-of-coverage-attorneys-in-assembly-line-litigation/ ; HLS listing: https://hls.harvard.edu/bibliography/the-pervasive-and-troubling-use-of-coverage-attorneys-in-assembly-line-litigation/

This is the "April 2026 Harvard report on fast-moving debt cases" from the brief. Clarifications:
- **It is a law review article by a single author**, not a clinic "report." Press coverage on **April 27, 2026** called it a report and said it was to be published in May. — GBH News, https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2026-04-27/placeholder-attorneys-fuel-unfair-debt-wins-in-mass-courts-new-report-finds ; trade press headline "Report Flags Risks in Fast-Moving Debt Collection Cases in Small Claims Courts," https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2026/04/27/report-flags-risks-in-fast-moving-debt-collection-cases-in-small-claims-courts/
- **Method** (per the Georgetown abstract): interviews with industry actors. No case-count statistics are given in the abstract.
- **Findings** (per the abstract):
  - Debt-collection "mills" hire substitute "coverage" or "appearance" attorneys to make most court appearances.
  - The practice is largely unique to collection cases.
  - It raises multiple professional-responsibility problems.
  - The article calls for reform and enforcement.
- **Press-reported details** (**UNVERIFIED** against the article text):
  - Coverage attorneys handle up to 70 cases a day.
  - Courts are "extremely passive."
  - "Clear ethical violations."
  - More than 146,000 consumer debt cases were filed in Massachusetts in 2025, up more than 60% in two years (attributed by GBH to Massachusetts Trial Court data).
- The SSRN draft (abstract_id=5468491) was behind a bot check and was not read.
- **Relevance:** this is about defendants in creditor-filed small claims, not individual plaintiffs collecting.

## Part 4. What this means for the site ("Is it worth it?" calculator)

These are plain-language takeaways. The probability ranges are **our editorial synthesis** of the studies above, not findings from any one study, and should be labeled that way on the site.

1. **Default probability of collecting something.** A reasonable default range is about **45–55%** for a typical individual-vs-individual small claims win. Denver found 45% collected something; Michigan found 56% got full or partial payment. Expected **dollar recovery** is lower: Denver found **about 31% of awarded dollars** collected, and the Dutch study found about half after 3 years. A cautious default for "expected value" is **about 30–50% of the judgment**, before collection costs.
2. **Show the costs of collecting.** In CA, a single round of enforcement typically costs **$40 (writ) + $45–$50 (levy)**, plus $60 + service if a debtor's exam is needed. These costs are usually recoverable from the debtor, but only if the debtor has money. Add them to the "cost" side and to the judgment.
3. **Factors that raise the odds** (consistent with how collection tools work, not separately measured):
   - The defendant has a **steady W-2 job**. Wage garnishment works, but it's capped: nothing below $811.20/week in CA in 2026, then up to 20%.
   - The defendant has a **bank account with more than the exemption** ($2,325 in CA from July 1, 2026; more if it holds federal benefits).
   - The defendant **owns real property**. An abstract creates a lien that gets paid on sale or refinance.
     - **Landlords** (e.g., security-deposit cases) usually own real property, and rental property isn't protected by the homestead exemption. That makes them among the most collectible defendants.
     - In Pennsylvania, landlord residential-lease judgments are an exception to the wage-garnishment ban (that exception helps landlords, not tenants).
   - The defendant is an **operating business** with receivables, a cash register, or a business account (till tap or keeper levy, bank levy).
   - The judgment came from a **California car accident** (the DMV license-suspension lever).
   - The case was **mediated or settled** rather than adjudicated: about 2× compliance in Maine; 79% vs. 56% in Michigan.
   - The defendant **appeared and has reputational, licensing, or business reasons** to pay.
4. **Factors that lower the odds:**
   - The defendant is **"judgment-proof"**: income only from exempt sources (SSI, Social Security, unemployment, public benefits), low wages under the garnishment floor, no non-exempt property.
   - The defendant has **no known employer or bank**, moves often, or can't be located.
   - The defendant **lives out of state** (CA service and enforcement get much harder).
   - The defendant is in a **state that bars wage garnishment** for the debt type (TX; PA except listed exceptions; NC with family-support affidavit; SC for consumer-credit debts).
   - The defendant has **other garnishments or tax/support orders**, which take priority.
   - The business is **closed or dissolved**.
5. **Time horizon.** CA judgments last 10 years and can be renewed, and earn 10% interest (5% for qualifying personal or medical debt). Today's "judgment-proof" defendant may become collectible later. The calculator can mention this without promising it.
6. **Be honest in copy.** Say clearly that **the court does not collect for you** and that published studies found roughly **half of winners never collect anything**. Use the Denver figure as the anchor and the Maine survey as a supporting anecdote, labeled as such.
